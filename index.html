Chitragupta · Android app

Privacy policy

Effective 24 November 2026

This is the privacy policy for the Android app Chitragupta (package in.adepts.chitragupta). The app is made by Adepts Cloud, based in India. Questions about privacy go to adeptscloud@gmail.com.

The short version

  • Your data stays on your phone.
  • The app has no permission to use the internet. Android itself blocks every network connection the app might try to make.
  • There is no account, no sign-in, no server, no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting.
  • The developer does not receive, see, sell or share what you keep in the app.

The rest of this page says the same thing in detail.

What the app keeps on your phone

Everything is in one database in the app's private storage on your phone.

  • What you type in. Your accounts (subscriptions, loans, credit cards, bills and so on), amounts, due dates, payments, notes and settings. If you choose to enter them, also a loan you have in mind, your bank's interest rates and your own credit score. The app never looks a credit score up: it has no way to.
  • Figures read from bank SMS, if you switch that on. See the next section.
  • App lock settings, if you switch the lock on. An app PIN is kept only as a salted one-way hash. The PIN itself is not stored.
  • A backup key, if you set a backup password. The password itself is not stored. See "Backups and the backup password" below.

Bank SMS

Reading SMS is off until you switch it on. When you do, the app first shows you what it will read and why, and asks you to agree. Android then asks for its own permission. If you say no to either, nothing is read.

What is read
Messages from named senders, the kind banks and card issuers use. Messages from ordinary phone numbers are skipped. A message that is a one-time password is never read further.
Where it is read
On your phone only. The app cannot send it anywhere.
When it is read
When the app is open, and also while the app is closed: when a new message arrives, Android wakes the app for a moment so it can read it.
What is kept
For each bank message the app keeps a fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash, which cannot be turned back into the message) and the figures it read: the amount, the date, the last digits of the card or account, the merchant's name and the bank. The text of the message is not stored.
What it is used for
To enter card bills and payments for you. To build a list of what you spent, with categories: card purchases, UPI and account debits, cash withdrawals, and money received. To raise savings alerts from that list, such as a subscription the app does not know, a charge made twice, a price rise, an EMI that changed, a bounced auto-debit, or a fee.
What it is not used for
Advertising, profiling, or anything outside the app. It is not shared with anyone and not sold.
Switching it off
Switch Bank SMS off in the app at any time. Reading stops, and the app is no longer woken when messages arrive. You can also take the permission away in Android's settings. What was already entered stays in the app until you delete it. "Forget the spending that was read", on the Bank SMS page, removes every spending entry that came from a message.

The app does not have the internet permission. It does not ask for your contacts, location, camera, microphone, call log or files.

What leaves your phone

Nothing leaves your phone through the app itself. It has no way to send anything.

A copy installed from Google Play carries the purchase check described above. That check works through the Play Store app, which is a separate app with its own internet access and is covered by Google's privacy policy. Chitragupta itself still cannot connect to anything.

Data leaves your phone only when you move it:

  • Backups. A backup is a complete copy of the app's database. One is made only when you save one, or when you link a file for automatic copies. You choose where it goes through Android's own file picker. If you choose a cloud app such as Google Drive, that app does the uploading, and what happens to the file there is covered by that service's privacy policy, not this one.
  • Moving to a new phone. Android's cloud backup of the app is switched off. Copying phone to phone with a cable may still move the app's data to your new phone.

Backups and the backup password

You can set a backup password. With one set, every backup file is encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key is made from your password. The password is stored nowhere, so a forgotten password cannot be recovered, by you or by the developer.

Without a backup password, a backup file is not encrypted. Anyone who gets the file can read it. Keep such files somewhere private.

What the developer receives

From the app: nothing. The developer has no server and no copy of your data.

Two things happen outside the app:

  • Buying the app. Google Play handles the purchase. Google gives the developer limited order details for accounting and refunds. What Google collects when you buy or install an app is covered by Google's privacy policy.
  • Writing to the developer. If you send an email, the developer sees your address and what you wrote, and uses them only to answer you.

Sharing with others

The developer shares no user data with anyone, because the developer has none. The app contains no advertising or analytics software.

How the data is protected

  • The database is in the app's private storage. Android keeps other apps out of it.
  • An optional app lock: the phone's own fingerprint, face or screen lock, or a 4 to 6 digit app PIN. After 5 wrong PINs the app makes you wait, up to 15 minutes.
  • With the lock on, the app's screen is hidden in the list of recent apps and screenshots are blocked. You can switch this off.
  • With the lock on, home-screen widgets hide amounts and names.
  • On a locked phone, a reminder shows that there is a reminder, not what it says.
  • Backup files can be encrypted with your password, as described above.

This protection has limits. The main one: the database on the phone is not encrypted by the app itself. It relies on Android's protection of app storage and on the phone's own encryption. Someone who has your unlocked phone, with no app lock set, can open the app and read it.

How long data is kept, and deleting it

Data stays on your phone until you delete it. Nothing is kept anywhere else by the developer, so there is nothing for the developer to delete.

To remove everything from the phone, do either of these:

  • Uninstall the app.
  • Open Android Settings, then Apps, then Chitragupta, then Storage, then Clear storage.

Backup files you saved elsewhere are yours. Delete them where you put them.

Children

The app is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Indian law

The developer is in India. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is about personal data that an organisation collects and processes. Through this app the developer collects none: your data is processed on your own phone, by you. If you believe the developer holds personal data about you, for example from an email you sent, write to the address below and it will be corrected or deleted.
